How safe are our roads?

With 54,641 fatal crashes recorded across Australia, the data paints a clear picture of when, where, and to whom our roads are most dangerous. Saturdays are the deadliest day, accounting for 9,987 crashes (18.3% of the total), and when combined with Friday's 8,973 (16.4%), the two days alone make up over a third of all fatal crashes — a pattern strongly linked to weekend nightlife, fatigue, and impaired driving. Monday is the safest day with just 6,299 crashes (11.5%). Risk also surges during holiday periods, with the Christmas window recording significantly more fatal crashes than Easter, driven by longer travel distances and increased alcohol consumption. Seasonal trends reinforce this, as fatalities peak in December and January during the Australian summer when more people are on the roads. Despite improvements in vehicle safety and road infrastructure, there is no strong long-term decline in fatal crashes across the years in this dataset, suggesting that distracted driving, speed, and drug-impaired driving may be offsetting those gains. Motorcycle riders, pedestrians, and pedal cyclists face disproportionate risk relative to their presence on the road, reminding us that road safety must extend beyond drivers to protect the most vulnerable users.
