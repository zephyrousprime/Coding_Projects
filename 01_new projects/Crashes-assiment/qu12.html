Saturday is the most dangerous day to drive

Saturday records the highest number of road deaths in the dataset at 9,987 (18.3% of all records), followed by Friday at 8,973 (16.4%). Together, these two days account for over a third of all road deaths. Monday is the lowest with just 6,299 (11.5%). There is a visible trend of increasing deaths through the week, which drops on Sunday — Sunday has fewer deaths than Saturday and Friday but not Thursday. However, the dataset alone cannot determine why Sunday is lower, as factors such as traffic volume, travel distance, speeding, alcohol use and road conditions are not directly compared in this analysis.

The Christmas period records more road deaths than Easter

The Christmas period (12 days from 23 December) records 1,714 road deaths compared to Easter's 290 (5 days from the Thursday before Good Friday). When adjusted for the different period lengths, Christmas averages approximately 142.8 deaths per day while Easter averages 58.0 per day — meaning the daily rate during Christmas is roughly 2.5 times higher. However, this still does not measure individual driving risk because traffic volume and kilometres travelled during each period are not included in the dataset.

Data source: Australian Road Deaths Database — data.gov.au
